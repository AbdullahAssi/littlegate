# Android Platform Feasibility and Google Play Policy Constraints for Littlegate (state as of 2026-10-22)

Dating convention: most Play Console Help policy pages show no "last updated" date. For those pages, "page undated, fetched 2026-10-22" means the text was read on that day. Effective or deadline dates are given wherever the page states one. Anything known only from recall is kept out of Cited Findings and goes under Inferences or Gaps.

## 1. Google Play policy risk for the current design (Accessibility, VPN, Families, permissions, FGS, Stalkerware, Device Admin)

### Takeaway
Every API in the baseline design is permitted on Play for parental control, but each one needs its own Play Console declaration plus a demo video, and most also need a separate in-app prominent-disclosure screen. The policy problems are specific ones: Littlegate cannot claim `isAccessibilityTool`, must not declare `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` or `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, and must never use device-admin or DPM powers to stop the app being removed without a clear removal path. A ransomware or "elevated privilege abuse" finding is the most serious way this kind of app gets removed from Play.

### Cited Findings

**AccessibilityService API policy** (page undated, fetched 2026-10-22; the only date on the page is 2021-11-03, when apps targeting API 31 had to complete the declaration and get approval)
- Only services that help people with disabilities may set `isAccessibilityTool`. The listed non-qualifying examples include "antivirus software, automation tools, assistants, monitoring apps, cleaners, password managers, and launchers." Littlegate is a monitoring app with a launcher, so it cannot claim the flag. — [Play: Use of the AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- Apps may not autonomously "initiate, plan, and execute actions or decisions." Deterministic, rule-based automation that follows a static, human-defined script ("If Trigger X occurs, perform Action Y") is explicitly not prohibited. — [Play: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- Apps that are not accessibility tools need an in-app prominent disclosure. It must appear during normal use (not in menus), describe the data accessed and how it is used, require an affirmative action, not live only in the privacy policy or ToS, and be separate from other data disclosures. — [Play: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- The Play Console declaration for non-tools asks for the purposes of use, whether personal data is collected or shared, the data types, and a video. The video must show the app opening, the path to the disclosure, the full disclosure text, both the consent and non-consent flows, and a core feature. Changing how the app uses the API means resubmitting. Deceptive or undeclared use can lead to suspension or developer account termination. — [Play: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- The policy page does not mention parental control at all. Parental control is neither named as allowed nor named as banned. — [Play: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)

**VpnService policy** (page undated, fetched 2026-10-22; declaration opened 2022-11-01 and was required by 2023-08-31)
- VpnService is allowed where VPN is the core functionality. The listed exceptions include "Parental control and enterprise management apps", "App usage tracking", device security apps such as firewalls, and others. Ad blocking and DNS filtering are not named. — [Play: VpnService policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12564964?hl=en)
- Prohibited: collecting personal or sensitive data without prominent disclosure and consent, and redirecting or manipulating other apps' traffic for monetization (example: routing ad traffic through another country). — [Play: VpnService policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12564964?hl=en)
- Apps "must encrypt the data from the device to the VPN tunnel endpoint", must document VpnService use in the Play listing, and must submit the Play Console declaration. The declaration asks whether VPN is the core function, which permitted function applies, for two videos of 90 seconds or less (the VPN in use, and the disclosure/consent flow), what data is collected, and whether traffic is redirected for monetization. — [Play: VpnService policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12564964?hl=en)

**Stalkerware and Monitoring Applications** (page undated, footer "(c)2026 Google", fetched 2026-10-22)
- Prohibited: apps that collect personal or sensitive data from a device and send it to a third party for monitoring. The exception covers apps designed and marketed exclusively for parents monitoring their children, or for enterprises monitoring employees, and only if they meet all the requirements. Tracking a spouse is never allowed, even with consent. — [Play: Privacy, Deception and Device Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888380)
- Requirements: don't position the app as spying or covert surveillance; don't hide tracking; show a **persistent notification while running** and use a **unique icon**; disclose all monitoring in the Play description; don't link to non-compliant APKs off Play; comply with local law; set the `IsMonitoringTool` manifest flag. — [Play: Privacy, Deception and Device Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888380)
- The `isMonitoringTool` meta-data must be present in every version code on every track. Allowed values are `child_monitoring`, `enterprise_management` and `other`. Play checks the declared value against the listing category, and **Google Play Protect warns users when a monitoring app is on their device**. — [Play: isMonitoringTool flag](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955211?hl=en)

**Device Admin / anti-removal**
- The Ransomware section lists using device admin features to block removal by the user as an example of ransomware. Its key considerations are: get explicit consent for device management, don't use device admin to block uninstallation, and provide a clear way to remove the app. "Elevated Privilege Abuse" separately lists apps that abuse features to prevent uninstallation or stopping. — [Play: Privacy, Deception and Device Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888380)
- On the platform side, legacy (non-DO/PO) device admin policies are being deprecated. For example, `setCameraDisabled` via `USES_POLICY_DISABLE_CAMERA` "is deprecated for legacy device admins since Q". Legacy admins that target Q get a `SecurityException`. — [DevicePolicyManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)

**QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES** (page undated, fetched 2026-10-22)
- Allowed only when the app's core user-facing function needs broad visibility. The listed examples are "device search, antivirus apps, file managers, and browsers". Temporary exceptions exist for real-money gambling and regulated financial apps; personal loan apps are excluded. **Parental control and launchers are not mentioned.** The developer must show that a less intrusive visibility method would not work. — [Play: QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10158779?hl=en)

**Exact alarms** (page undated, fetched 2026-10-22)
- `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is acceptable only when "The app is an alarm or timer app" or "a calendar app that shows event notifications". Other exact-timing needs should use `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, which requires user approval. — [Play: Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241)
- `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is granted by the user, can be revoked by the user or the system, and has been denied by default since Android 14. Apps should check `canScheduleExactAlarms()`. — [Android: Schedule alarms](https://developer.android.com/training/scheduling/alarms); [Android 14: exact alarms denied by default](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)

**Foreground service types** (Play page undated, fetched 2026-10-22)
- For apps targeting Android 14+, every FGS type must be declared on the App content page with a description, the impact of deferral, a demo video and a use case. `specialUse` is allowed only in "limited scenarios", and "All foreground service types are subject to review." — [Play: Foreground service and full-screen intent requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)
- On the platform side, `specialUse` needs a free-form `PROPERTY_SPECIAL_USE_FGS_SUBTYPE` explanation, which Play reviews. `systemExempted` is allowed only if the app meets one of these: Device Owner, Profile Owner, **Device Admin app**, **holds SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM or USE_EXACT_ALARM**, **VPN app (configured in Settings > Network & Internet > VPN)**, demo mode, or ROLE_EMERGENCY. Otherwise the system throws `ForegroundServiceTypeNotAllowedException`. — [Android: Foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)
- Upcoming Play deadlines listed on the policy-deadlines page: 2026-08-26 (Families, Child Endangerment and Age-Restricted Content policies extended to anonymous and random chat apps); 2026-09-30 (Play Console registration for developer verification); 2027-01-27 (sensitive-permission changes, and geofencing removed as an approved FGS use case). — [Play: policy deadlines page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12253906?hl=en)
- Full-screen intent: from 2025-01-22, only calling or alarm apps targeting Android 14+ get `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT` by default. — [Play: FGS/FSI requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)

**Families policy**
- Apps whose target audience includes at least one children's age group must follow the Families policies. That includes using only Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs and avoiding APIs and SDKs not approved for child-directed services. Misrepresenting the target audience can lead to removal. — [Play: Families policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en); [Play: Target audience and app content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159)

### Inferences
- **Accessibility guard fits the "deterministic automation" carve-out.** "If foreground package is not on the allowlist, return to the kid launcher" is a static, human-defined rule, which is the explicitly permitted pattern. Do not add LLM or agent-like decision-making to the accessibility path. Risk for the current design: **Medium**. It is allowed, but reviewers reject apps over incomplete disclosure videos.
- **VPN encryption clause.** A local-only tunnel has no remote endpoint, so "encrypt device to tunnel endpoint" probably does not bite. Forwarding the filtered DNS upstream as plaintext UDP/53 could still draw a reviewer question. Using DoH or DoT upstream (to the chosen resolver) removes the question and matches the privacy stance. Declare as "Parental control" (not core VPN), say that no traffic data leaves the device, and state there is no monetization redirect. Risk: **Low to Medium**.
- **isMonitoringTool is an open decision, not a settled one.** The Stalkerware policy is triggered by collecting data and *transmitting it to a third party*. Littlegate v1 keeps everything local and the parent is present on the same phone, so the plain reading is that the flag is not required. Declaring `child_monitoring` brings Play Protect warnings, a persistent-notification requirement and listing requirements. Not declaring it is defensible only while nothing leaves the phone. If a remote parent dashboard is added later (sending child activity to a parent device), the flag and every Stalkerware requirement apply in full. A persistent notification is already needed for the VPN/FGS, so that part costs nothing extra.
- **QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES: don't declare it.** Parental control is not a named use, and a kid launcher can enumerate launchable apps with a `<queries>` element for `ACTION_MAIN`/`CATEGORY_LAUNCHER` intents. That is the "less intrusive method" the policy expects. Risk if declared: **High** (likely rejection). Risk with `<queries>`: **Low**. (Inference; the `<queries>` mechanism is from general platform knowledge, not fetched this session.)
- **Exact alarms.** Littlegate is not an alarm, timer or calendar app, so `USE_EXACT_ALARM` would very likely be rejected. Use `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` with an in-app grant flow, and fall back to inexact alarms or WorkManager if it is denied or revoked. Also consider whether schedule edges really need minute precision: `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` versus `setWindow`.
- **FGS type choice.** The VPN service itself runs as a `VpnService`, and the VPN is exempt from background limits while it is active. If a separate guard or scheduler FGS is needed, Littlegate qualifies for `systemExempted` once it is the active VPN app or holds `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` (or is DO / device admin in Lock mode). That avoids the free-form `specialUse` review. Whether Play still asks for a `systemExempted` justification is unconfirmed (see Gaps).
- **Families policy exposure.** If the Play target audience is set to "18+ (parents)" and the Kid Mode launcher is described as a feature parents operate, the Families policy should not apply. If the listing or target audience includes under-13s (for example, marketing the kid launcher to children), all Families requirements apply. Keep the store listing parent-facing.
- **What gets parental apps rejected or removed** (synthesis from the policy text above): (1) missing or weak prominent disclosure, or a video that doesn't show the non-consent flow, for Accessibility or VPN; (2) undeclared or over-broad permissions such as QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES or USE_EXACT_ALARM; (3) anti-uninstall via device admin or accessibility without a clear removal path (Ransomware / Elevated Privilege Abuse); (4) hidden-icon or "stealth" modes (Stalkerware); (5) marketing that implies spouse or adult tracking; (6) linking to off-Play APKs that unlock extra features; (7) changing API use without resubmitting the declaration.

### Gaps
- None of the Play pages fetched say whether `systemExempted` needs a Play Console FGS declaration. One third-party blog claims it is exempt, which is unverified.
- No first-party statement was found on whether `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` has a Play declaration form. I believe it does not (it is a special app access granted by the user), but this was not verified this session.
- No Play policy text was found specifically on `NotificationListenerService` or on Lock-Task / Device Owner apps distributed through Play.
- The current Families-policy page body was not fetched in full; the findings come from search summaries of the Play Help pages.

## 2. Android 15 / 16 / 17 changes affecting blockers

### Takeaway
Three platform changes matter most. First, **Advanced Protection on Android 17 revokes accessibility from non-accessibility-tool apps**, which kills the AccessibilityService guard on those devices. Second, **developer verification** (enforced from 2026-09-30 in BR/ID/SG/TH, globally in 2027) and the expanded **restricted settings** for sideloaded apps make "sideload to get around Play" unattractive. Third, **Chrome Secure DNS / Private DNS / Private Space** create DNS and profile bypasses that only a Device Owner can fully close. Android 16 also ships Google's own **built-in PIN-protected Parental Controls**, a direct free competitor.

### Cited Findings

**Advanced Protection Mode (AAPM) and accessibility (Android 17)**
- Secondary reports (Android 17 Beta 2, March 2026; Google announcement reported October 2026) say that when Advanced Protection is on, AccessibilityService access is limited to verified `isAccessibilityTool="true"` apps. The system prevents granting it to other apps and **automatically revokes it from already-installed apps**. Monitoring apps and launchers are named as losing access. — [Android Authority: Beta 2 AAPM accessibility](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-17-beta-2-advanced-protection-mode-accessibility-apps-3648860); [The Hacker News, March 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/android-17-blocks-non-accessibility.html); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/189497/security/advanced-protection-mode-in-android-17-prevents-apps-from-misusing-accessibility-services.html); [Android Authority teardown](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-advanced-protection-mode-accessibility-apk-teardown-3640742/). *All of these are secondary; I could not reach Google's primary announcement (The Hacker News October 2026 article returned HTTP 403).*
- First-party API: `android.security.advancedprotection.AdvancedProtectionManager` offers `isAdvancedProtectionEnabled()` and `registerAdvancedProtectionCallback(Executor, Callback)` for detecting the mode. — [AdvancedProtectionManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/security/advancedprotection/AdvancedProtectionManager)

**Android 17 (API 37) behavior changes**
- For apps targeting 37: BAL (background activity launch) restrictions now cover `IntentSender`. Apps must migrate from `MODE_BACKGROUND_ACTIVITY_START_ALLOWED` to `MODE_BACKGROUND_ACTIVITY_START_ALLOW_IF_VISIBLE`. — [Android 17 behavior changes (targeting 37)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17?hl=en)
- For apps targeting 37: background audio needs a running FGS with while-in-use capability, or the exact alarm permission when using `USAGE_ALARM` streams. Audio calls from an invalid lifecycle fail silently. — [Android 17 changes (targeting 37)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17?hl=en); [Android 17 changes (all apps)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all)
- For apps targeting 37: `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` runtime permission becomes mandatory; ECH (Encrypted Client Hello) is enabled for TLS by default; SMS OTPs are withheld from most apps for 3 hours; `setContentCaptureEnabled(false)` no longer disables content capture; accessibility services get `AccessibilityEvent.getTextChangeTypes()`. — [Android 17 behavior changes (targeting 37)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17?hl=en)
- The Android 17 "all apps" page lists no changes to VPN, DNS, usage stats, device admin, launcher or overlays (the page says it is a list of "some of the more important changes"). — [Android 17 changes (all apps)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all)

**Android 16 (API 36)**
- Android 16 deprecates accessibility announcements (`announceForAccessibility`, `TYPE_ANNOUNCEMENT`) and adds default hardening against Intent-redirection attacks. — [Android 16 behavior changes (all apps)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-all)
- **Built-in Parental Controls**: "available with the update for Android 16". They live in Settings > Parental Controls behind a PIN and offer a daily screen-time cap, nightly Downtime, per-app limits or blocks, and mature-content filters for Chrome and Google Search (article dated 2026-03-16). — [android.com: Get started with Android parental controls](https://www.android.com/articles/get-started-with-android-parental-controls/). Internet Matters says they were released in December 2025. — [Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/parental-controls/smartphones-and-other-devices/android-smartphone/). A hidden "Supervision" page with a PIN and web content filters was first spotted in April 2025. — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-16-supervision-settings-3546604)

**Android 15 (API 35)**
- `BOOT_COMPLETED` receivers may not launch `dataSync`, `camera`, `mediaPlayback`, `phoneCall`, `mediaProjection` or `microphone` FGS types. `specialUse` and `systemExempted` are not on that list. The `dataSync` FGS is capped at 6 hours per 24 hours for apps targeting 35. Holding `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` exempts an app from FGS background-start limits only while it has a visible overlay window. — [Android 15 behavior changes (targeting 35)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)
- **Private Space**: apps in it sit in a separate launcher container. They are "hidden from the recents view, notifications, settings, and from other apps when the private space is locked", and they are installed as separate copies with separate accounts and data. — [Android 15 features: Private space](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/features#private-space)
- `UserManager.DISALLOW_ADD_PRIVATE_PROFILE` (API 35): "For users with a device owner set, the default value is true and the device owner currently cannot change it to false." — [UserManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- **Restricted settings** (Android 13+) block sideloaded apps from sensitive permissions until the user taps "Allow restricted settings". Android 15's CDD expands the list to Accessibility, Notification listener, Device admin, Display over other apps, **Usage access**, Dialer/SMS roles and SMS. Apps installed through the session-based install API (typical for app stores) are not restricted. — [Android Authority: Android 15 restricted settings](https://androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/09/12/android-15-sideloaded-apps-restrictions/); [CleanBrowsing guide](https://cleanbrowsing.org/support/mobile/disable-restricted-settings-android). The final shipping behavior of "Enhanced Confirmation Mode" was not confirmed in these sources.

**Developer verification (sideloading)**
- From **2026-09-30**, users in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand on certified devices are protected from unregistered developers' apps; enforcement expands to all certified devices in **2027**. — [Android developer verification guide](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides?authuser=3); [Play Console: Understanding developer verification](https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/16561738?hl=en)
- Unverified apps can still be installed via ADB or an "advanced flow" (developer mode, restart, 24-hour wait, re-authentication). — [The Hacker News, June 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/google-sets-sept-30-deadline-for.html). Sources disagree on whether the Developer Verifier first appeared in April or June 2026. — [Android Authority timeline](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-developer-verification-rollout-sideloading-flow-3653395)

**DNS bypasses: Chrome Secure DNS, Private DNS**
- Chrome 85 (2020-09-02) brought Secure DNS (DoH) to Android. In automatic mode it upgrades to DoH only when the current provider is known to support it ("same-provider upgrade"), so family-safe filtering is preserved, and it falls back to the regular DNS. **Manual configuration lets users pick a specific DoH provider without fallback.** "Chrome will disable Secure DNS if it detects a managed environment via the presence of one or more enterprise policies." — [Chromium Blog, 2020-09-02](https://blog.chromium.org/2020/09/a-safer-and-more-private-browsing.html)
- `DISALLOW_CONFIG_PRIVATE_DNS` (API 29) "can only be set by a device owner or a profile owner of an organization-owned managed profile". `setGlobalPrivateDnsModeSpecifiedHost` (API 29) is device-owner only. Its docs warn: "In case a VPN is used in conjunction with Private DNS resolver, the Private DNS resolver must be reachable both from within and outside the VPN… system traffic to the resolver may not go through the VPN." — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager); [DevicePolicyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)
- An AOSP commit fixed strict-mode Private DNS validation on VPNs; NetworkMonitor now resolves the Private DNS hostname on VPN networks too. — [AOSP commit 0929eb9](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/0929eb9%5E%21)
- Apps with their own DNS clients or DoH can bypass system DNS settings. — [ExpressVPN: Android Private DNS](https://expressvpn.com/blog/android-private-dns)

**Play Age Signals API**
- Beta. Returns age bands (default 0-12, 13-15, 16-17, 18+) only in regions where Play is legally required to provide them. Flow: `requestAgeSignalsAccess()` returns SHARED, NOT_SHARED or VERIFICATION_REQUIRED, then `checkAgeSignals()`. Android 6.0+. — [Android: Request age signals](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/request-age-signals); [Use Age Signals API v3](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/v3/use-age-signals-api)
- Reported to have launched in Brazil on 2026-03-17 (Digital ECA). On 2026-07-29 Google said it would expand to all markets by the end of 2026, starting with Australia and Canada in mid-August. Signals reportedly come from Family Link age ranges and are off by default. — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/google-play-age-signals-api-global-rollout/); [Reclaim The Net](https://reclaimthenet.org/google-to-expand-play-age-signals-api-to-all-users-worldwide) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **The AAPM accessibility revocation is a kid-initiated bypass vector.** An older child who turns on Advanced Protection (Android 17, Settings > Security) would silently strip Littlegate's accessibility guard. Mitigations: (a) treat the UsageStatsManager polling guard as a first-class primary path, not a fallback. **Detection alone is not enough:** the poller runs in a VPN or FGS process, and the official background-activity-start exemption list does **not** include VpnService or foreground services. The listed exemptions are a visible window, the current IME, a system-sent PendingIntent such as a notification tap, **user-granted SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW**, START_ACTIVITIES_FROM_BACKGROUND, being bound by a privileged service, launches "initiated by the device's launcher app", and core OS components ([Android: Restrictions on starting activities from the background](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/background-starts)). Full-screen intents are restricted to call and alarm apps (see Section 1). So the non-accessibility guard needs **SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW**, either to draw a blocking overlay or for its BAL exemption. That permission is a restricted setting for sideloaded apps, and on Android 15 its FGS-start exemption needs a visible overlay. Whether Littlegate's ROLE_HOME status covers programmatic self-launches (beyond user taps) is unverified (see Gaps); (b) detect AAPM with `AdvancedProtectionManager.registerAdvancedProtectionCallback` and show the parent an alert or lock Kid Mode; (c) gate Settings via the allowlist so the child cannot reach Security settings in Kid Mode; (d) in Lock mode, use DO policies that don't depend on accessibility.
- **Chrome DoH and the local VPN DNS filter.** If the VPN advertises a private virtual DNS IP (for example 10.x), automatic same-provider upgrade should not trigger, because the "provider" is unknown. Manual Secure DNS (for example dns.google) or a third-party browser with built-in DoH bypasses the DNS filter. Mitigations, all needing testing: block well-known DoH/DoT hostnames (dns.google, cloudflare-dns.com, dns.quad9.net, doh.opendns.com, etc.) in the filter; route the full IPv4/IPv6 default route through the tunnel and drop TCP/UDP 853 and known DoH resolver IPs; drop QUIC (UDP 443) to resolver IPs; keep Chrome Settings and third-party browsers off the Kid Mode allowlist. In Lock mode a Device Owner can push Chrome managed configuration (`DnsOverHttpsMode=off`) through application restrictions, and the blog says enterprise policies disable Secure DNS. (Inference: the DO-to-Chrome app-restrictions path was not verified this session.)
- **Private DNS.** With a DNS-only tunnel (only the virtual DNS IP routed), strict-mode Private DNS (DoT, port 853) to a third-party resolver would not enter the tunnel and would bypass filtering. In non-DO mode Littlegate cannot stop the user changing Private DNS; it can only detect it (`LinkProperties.isPrivateDnsActive()` / `getPrivateDnsServerName()`) and keep Settings > Network off the allowlist. In Lock mode, `DISALLOW_CONFIG_PRIVATE_DNS` closes this.
- **Private Space bypass (non-DO).** On Android 15+, a child with the unlock credential, or a parent's own Private Space, can run apps in a separate profile. The main-profile accessibility service and UsageStats may not see those apps, and a main-user VpnService may not cover the private profile's traffic (see Gaps). Without a Device Owner the creation of Private Space cannot be blocked (`DISALLOW_ADD_PRIVATE_PROFILE` needs DO, and DO devices block it by default). Practical non-DO mitigation: Kid Mode's launcher simply doesn't expose the Private Space container, and Settings stays off the allowlist. This is a **headline weakness** of shared-phone Kid Mode that Lock mode fixes.
- **Developer verification.** Distributing outside Play doesn't escape scrutiny: from 2027 the developer must still register and verify. Sideloaded installs also hit restricted settings for Accessibility, Usage access, Device admin and overlays, which hurts onboarding. Play is effectively the only viable distribution route.
- **Android 16 built-in Parental Controls** largely covers Littlegate's baseline (daily cap, downtime, app limits, Chrome/Search filtering, PIN) for free. Differentiation has to come from category DNS filtering beyond Chrome/Search, the kid launcher and allowlist UX, YouTube Restricted Mode, privacy (no Google account), and support for pre-Android-16 devices.
- **Age Signals** is not useful to Littlegate as a control mechanism. It is for apps that need to know a user's age. It could be used opportunistically in regulated markets to suggest presets (low value, low risk).
- **ECH (Android 17).** ECH hides SNI but not DNS queries. A DNS-based filter still sees hostnames unless the browser or app also uses DoH. SNI-based filtering would become unreliable if it were ever added.

### Gaps
- No primary Google source (blog or docs page) was reachable for the Android 17 AAPM accessibility restriction. Final behavior, and whether it applies only to non-tool apps installed from any source, needs confirmation. The API level that `AdvancedProtectionManager` was added in was not visible in the fetched excerpt (it is believed to be Android 16, which is unverified).
- **Not verified:** whether a VpnService started in the main user covers Private Space and work-profile traffic (per-user VPN semantics), and whether main-profile UsageStatsManager or AccessibilityService see events from Private Space apps. This needs testing on a device.
- I found no first-party source on Chrome's Android behavior with a *local* VPN-provided DNS server, or on DNS-over-QUIC in Chrome for Android.
- Android 16 QPR and Android 17 changes to restricted settings / Enhanced Confirmation Mode were not confirmed.
- Not verified: whether the "launch initiated by the device's launcher app" BAL exemption lets the default HOME app bring itself (or a block screen) to the front programmatically from a background service, as opposed to user-tap launches. Needs a device test on Android 15–17.

## 3. Advanced features the APIs allow (time budgets, bedtime, notifications, installs, camera/mic, Device Owner, kiosk, work profile)

### Takeaway
Without Device Owner, Littlegate can enforce per-app daily budgets (UsageStats/UsageEvents plus the guard), schedules, notification suppression (NotificationListener) and allowlisting. It cannot reliably block installs, Private DNS changes, safe boot, factory reset or new users. With Device Owner (provisioning is believed to need a freshly reset device or adb, which is from recall and unverified, see Inferences), nearly everything becomes enforceable: lock task kiosk, app hiding or suspension, uninstall blocking, always-on VPN with lockdown, DNS and Private DNS lock, camera disable, and user restrictions. All of these are documented in DevicePolicyManager and UserManager.

### Cited Findings (verified against developer.android.com reference pages, fetched 2026-10-22)
- `DISALLOW_SAFE_BOOT` (API 23): set by device owner, profile owner on the primary user, or org-owned profile owner on the parent profile. — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET` (API 21): set by device owners and profile owners on an admin user; it blocks factory reset from Settings. — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_CONFIG_PRIVATE_DNS` (API 29): device owner or org-owned profile owner only; applies globally. — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_ADD_USER` (API 21): device owners or profile owners on the main user. `DISALLOW_ADD_PRIVATE_PROFILE` (API 35): defaults to true on DO devices. — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_INSTALL_APPS` (API 18) "also prevents device owners and profile owners installing apps". `DISALLOW_INSTALL_UNKNOWN_SOURCES` (API 18) excludes adb and "registered app stores" (a separate restriction covers those). `DISALLOW_UNINSTALL_APPS` (API 18). — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_CONFIG_VPN` (API 21): also prevents VPNs from starting, but since API 24 the system still starts always-on VPNs configured by the DO/PO. From API 31, enforcing it clears a user-configured active VPN. — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES` (API 21), `DISALLOW_CONFIG_DATE_TIME` (API 28, which stops clock changes used to cheat time limits), `DISALLOW_USB_FILE_TRANSFER` (API 18), `DISALLOW_MODIFY_ACCOUNTS` (API 18), `DISALLOW_CAMERA_TOGGLE` (API 31, device owner only). — [UserManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/UserManager)
- `setAlwaysOnVpnPackage(admin, pkg, lockdownEnabled)` (API 24): device or profile owner. The connection is auto-granted and persists across reboot. The VPN app must target 24+ and must not opt out via `SERVICE_META_DATA_SUPPORTS_ALWAYS_ON`. — [DevicePolicyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)
- `setLockTaskPackages` (API 21) and `setLockTaskFeatures` (API 28; by default only global actions are enabled, all other system features disabled): device owner, affiliated PO, or PO when no DO is set. — [DevicePolicyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)
- `setApplicationHidden` (API 21): package unavailable but data kept. `setUninstallBlocked` (API 21). `setUserControlDisabledPackages` (API 30): user cannot clear data or force-stop, and the packages are exempt from App Standby Buckets. `setPermittedAccessibilityServices` (API 21): restrict which accessibility services can be enabled. `setPermissionGrantState` (API 23): lock runtime permission grants. `setCameraDisabled` (API 14; legacy device-admin use deprecated since Q; on the newest release also blocks USB cameras). `setStatusBarDisabled` / `setKeyguardDisabled` (API 23). — [DevicePolicyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)
- A new generic `setPolicy(PolicyIdentifier, scope, value)` API appears as "Added in version 37.2". — [DevicePolicyManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/admin/DevicePolicyManager)

### Inferences — candidate feature matrix
(Risk ratings are my judgment based on the policy text in Section 1. Effort is native Kotlin effort for one experienced dev: S = under 1 week, M = 1–3 weeks, L = more than 3 weeks.)

Third parties must build on UsageStats, accessibility, overlays, VpnService and DPM.

## 4. On-device ML feasibility (image/nudity classification, text classification via accessibility)

### Takeaway
Gemini Nano (ML Kit GenAI / AICore) **cannot** be used for background content scanning. It only runs while the app is the top foreground app, it is quota-limited, and it is device-limited. On-device moderation would need a bundled TFLite/LiteRT classifier on frames captured by the app (screenshots need MediaProjection consent or accessibility `takeScreenshot`). Both bring a high policy and privacy-optics risk and real battery cost. Reading chat text through accessibility for classification is technically possible but is the highest-risk feature in this assessment.

### Cited Findings
- ML Kit GenAI APIs (Prompt, Image description, summarization, etc.) run on-device through AICore and Gemini Nano. Different devices run different Nano versions (`getBaseModelName()`). The supported-device list covers flagship models from Google, Samsung, OnePlus, vivo and others. — [ML Kit GenAI overview](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai)
- "GenAI API inference is permitted only when the app is the top foreground application. Using the API when the app is not in the foreground, including using a foreground service, will result in an ErrorCode.BACKGROUND_USE_BLOCKED response." AICore enforces per-app quotas (`BUSY`, `PER_APP_BATTERY_USE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`). — [ML Kit GenAI overview](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai)
- On a Pixel 9 Pro, image-to-text ran at about 510 tokens/s input and 11 tokens/s output, plus 0.8 s image encoding (May 2025 figures). — [Android Developers Blog, May 2025](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/05/on-device-gen-ai-apis-ml-kit-gemini-nano.html)
- Android 17 adds `getTextChangeTypes()` for accessibility `TYPE_VIEW_TEXT_CHANGED` events (text-diff metadata available to accessibility services). — [Android 17 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17?hl=en)
- Play's accessibility policy bans autonomous "plan and execute" behavior and requires disclosure of all data accessed. Monitoring apps cannot claim `isAccessibilityTool`. — [Play: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)

### Inferences
- **Gemini Nano**: unusable for real-time monitoring of other apps, because of the background block. It could only analyse content inside Littlegate's own UI, which has little value here.
- **Custom TFLite/LiteRT nudity classifier** (for example a MobileNet-class model of about 5–15 MB): runs in tens of milliseconds per frame on mid-range NPUs/GPUs. Continuous screen capture would need MediaProjection (a persistent capture indicator, user consent each session, and the `mediaProjection` FGS type, which cannot start from BOOT_COMPLETED on Android 15+) or periodic accessibility `takeScreenshot()` (API 30+). Expect noticeable battery drain at 1 fps or more. Risk: **High** (the optics of screen capture of a child, a Play reviewer asking about accessibility screenshots, and false positives). Effort: L. Recommendation: defer, or limit it to on-demand scanning of images in the kid's gallery via the photo picker / MediaStore (lower risk).
- **Chat text classification through accessibility**: feasible (read `AccessibilityNodeInfo` text from messaging apps). Play-wise it is "monitoring" with sensitive data. Locally only it doesn't trigger Stalkerware's third-party transmission clause, but it needs explicit disclosure. If alerts ever go to a remote parent, full Stalkerware requirements apply. It also stops working under AAPM. Risk: **High**. Effort: L. It also conflicts with the privacy-first brand.
- **Lower-risk ML**: on-device URL/domain categorisation of unknown domains (a small text classifier on hostnames, run inside the VPN process) to fill gaps in the blocklist. Risk: Low. Effort: M.

### Gaps
- No 2026 battery benchmarks were found for continuous on-device image classification with screen capture.
- It was not confirmed whether the ML Kit GenAI Image description API is available on the same device list as Prompt.
- No first-party docs were fetched on MediaProjection changes in Android 15–17 (per-session consent, single-app capture).

## 5. How competitors implement this on Android (Family Link, Qustodio, Kidslox)

### Takeaway
Third-party competitors use the same toolkit as Littlegate: accessibility, usage access, overlay, a local VPN, and device admin. Google's Family Link (and now the built-in Android 16 Parental Controls) relies on a privileged GMS component that third parties cannot get.

### Cited Findings
- **Qustodio**: uses Android's Accessibility setting to read and interact with screen content. It uses it to monitor browser content and app time and to block inappropriate content **and uninstallation**. "If accessibility is turned OFF, all monitoring will be disabled." — [Qustodio Help](https://help.qustodio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005219838-Why-and-how-does-Qustodio-use-Android-s-Accessibility-setting). It filters across 29 categories; filtering works at the DNS level on some platforms. — [Security.org review](https://www.security.org/parental-controls/qustodio/)
- **Kidslox**: uses Device Administrator privileges (its stated Android equivalent of iOS MDM), Accessibility (to prevent removal, see what the child views online, take screenshots, and require a PIN before uninstall), Usage Access, overlays (for "extra effective" app blocking), and a VPN service for content filtering. — [Kidslox: How does Kidslox work?](https://kidslox.com/guide-to/how-does-kidslox-work/); [Kidslox blog](https://kidslox.com/blog/how-does-kidslox-work)
- **Family Link / Google**: a GMS supervision package, `com.google.android.gms.supervision`, ships as a system/privileged app. — [Bayton sysapps DB](https://sysapps.bayton.org/packages/com.google.android.gms.supervision). Android 16 adds PIN-protected Parental Controls in Settings, and Family Link adds remote controls on top. — [android.com](https://www.android.com/articles/get-started-with-android-parental-controls/)
- Samsung (`com.samsung.android.app.parentalcare`) and TCL (`com.tcl.parentalcontrol`) also ship OEM parental-control system packages. — [Bayton sysapps: Samsung](https://sysapps.bayton.org/packages/com.samsung.android.app.parentalcare); [Bayton sysapps: TCL](https://sysapps.bayton.org/packages/com.tcl.parentalcontrol)

### Inferences
- The Qustodio and Kidslox pattern (accessibility PIN-gate on the uninstall screen) is how Play-distributed apps achieve "anti-uninstall" without DO. It works by intercepting the Settings/uninstall UI and demanding a PIN, so a removal path for the parent still exists. That keeps it within the Ransomware rule as long as the parent PIN path is clear. Littlegate can copy this (Med risk) and stay away from device-admin-based blocking.
- Littlegate's shared-phone Kid Mode (ROLE_HOME launcher) differs from these competitors, which assume a dedicated child device with a remote parent. That is a real differentiator and avoids the Stalkerware/remote-transmission obligations.
- All these competitors share the AAPM (Android 17) accessibility vulnerability. That makes the UsageStats-primary guard and the DO path competitive advantages if done well.

### Gaps
- No Family Link technical documentation describing its enforcement mechanism was found. The privileged-GMS conclusion is inferred from the system-app listing.
- Qustodio's and Kidslox's current (2026) Android filtering internals (local VPN vs. DNS profile, DoH handling) were not documented in first-party sources.
- No independent bypass testing of these competitors from 2025–2026 was found.
